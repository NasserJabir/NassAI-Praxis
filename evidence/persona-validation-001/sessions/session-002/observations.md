Specialized security/API observation recorded per protocol Session 002:

> External citation of validation evidence is an export-control problem, not a writing problem. Because publication is irreversible and externally untrusted, evidence should cross the public boundary only as claim-level statements backed by an internal claim-to-artifact index — raw transcripts stay internal and non-quotable, and sensitive values appear only as placeholders with location class.

This observation stays inside this session record. It is not claimed by another persona, not written to semantic/project memory, and not shaped into a pattern or proposal (reserved for later sessions under the protocol's human gate).

## Lessons

- The trust boundary, not the artifact, determines handling: identical content needs pointer-grade precision internally but existence-plus-risk framing externally. One placeholder discipline serves both at different precisions.
- Publication is the widest persistence surface; "what must never be persisted?" applied to a public page yields stricter rules than the same question applied to internal logs.
- Credibility claims survive without exposure when method/scope/outcome are stated and every public sentence maps to auditable internal evidence.
- Default classification (`internal`) plus proposal-then-review gives external release a natural default state: unpublished until explicitly signed off.
- Global automation mandates can collide with isolation scopes: post-task evaluation scripts would write to `evolve/` and persona files, which this session's permitted writes forbid. Skipping them and recording that choice here is the compliant resolution.
